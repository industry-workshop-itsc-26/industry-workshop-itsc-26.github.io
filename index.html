Overview

The 16th Workshop and Industry Panel on Cooperative Automated Driving will be held in conjunction with the 29th IEEE International Conference on Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITSC 2026). IEEE ITSC 2026 takes place in Naples, Italy, from 16 to 18 September; its workshops are scheduled for the preceding day - 15th September 2026.

The full-day workshop brings together researchers, industry experts, and public-sector stakeholders to discuss the technologies, deployment challenges, and partnerships needed for cooperative automated driving.

Workshop date 15 September 2026
Conference venue Convention Bureau Stazione Marittima
Host city Naples, Italy

Topics

The workshop is built around topics of shared relevance to academia, industry, and public authorities.

  • Cooperative perception, planning, and control
  • V2X communication and connected-vehicle systems
  • Safe, trustworthy, and certifiable automated driving
  • AI-enabled development, simulation, and testing
  • Infrastructure, fleet operation, and traffic impacts
  • Standards, regulation, and deployment pathways
  • Data-driven mobility planning and public infrastructure
  • New cooperation concepts across road, air, and space systems

Description

Cooperative automated driving depends on more than capable individual vehicles. It requires vehicles, road infrastructure, operators, and coordinating bodies to exchange information and act together reliably. This workshop explores the technical and organizational foundations for that cooperation, from perception and communication to safety assurance, regulation, and real-world deployment.

The programme combines invited presentations with an industry panel and discussion. It is intended for researchers and practitioners working on connected, cooperative, and automated mobility systems.

Programme elements

Invited presentations, a panel discussion, audience Q&A, and networking.

Researchers, industry experts, public authorities, and mobility-system stakeholders.

Dr. Kangwon Lee

Republic of Korea

From C/C++ to Rust for Automated Driving: Compiler Qualification and a Physical Testbed

Bio

Kangwon Lee, Ph.D., is an Associate Professor at TU Korea and Chair of the IEEE Intelligent Transportation Systems Society (ITSS) Korea Chapter. His work focuses on compiler safety and verification for safety-critical automotive software, with particular attention to transitioning from C/C++ to Rust for automated driving. He studies how ISO 26262-qualified Rust toolchains can keep in-vehicle control code within verifiable, human-auditable safety envelopes, and validates these ideas on a physical testbed built on the RoboRacer.ai platform. Building on this safety focus, he also develops open, auto-graded, LLM-tutored exercises on the RoboRacer / F1TENTH platform to lower the barrier for new researchers entering automated-driving software. At ITSC 2026 he also organizes a half-day workshop, "Compiler Safety and Verification for Intelligent Transportation Systems: From C/C++ to Rust."
